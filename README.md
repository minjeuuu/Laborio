### World 1: Hours of Work, Overtime, Weekly Rest & Night Shift Differential
* **Statutory Basis**: Labor Code Articles 82 to 90; Omnibus Rules Implementing the Labor Code, Book III, Rule I; DOLE Advisory No. 02, Series of 2004 (Compressed Workweek).
* **Comprehensive Topic Coverage**:
  * **Exempt Employees (Art. 82)**: Government workers (Civil Service rules), managerial employees (primary duty is management, regularly direct 2+ employees, hire/fire authority), officers/members of managerial staff (exercise discretion and independent judgment), field personnel (perform duties away from principal place and whose hours cannot be determined with reasonable certainty), family members dependent on employer, domestic workers / Kasambahay (governed by R.A. 10361), persons in personal service of another, and workers paid by results (piece-rate, takay, pakyao).
  * **Normal Hours of Work (Art. 83)**: 8 hours maximum per work day. Special rule for health personnel in cities/municipalities with at least 1,000,000 population or hospitals with at least 100-bed capacity: 40-hour workweek (8 hours/day for 5 days), with extra 8 hours paid at regular rate plus 30% overtime.
  * **Compressed Workweek (CWW Scheme)**: Voluntary bilateral agreement, maximum work hours of 12 hours/day without daily overtime pay provided weekly hours do not exceed 48 hours; health and safety safeguards under DOLE Advisory No. 02-04.
  * **Hours Worked & Compensable Working Time (Art. 84)**: All time during which employee is required to be on duty or at prescribed workplace, and all time during which employee is suffered or permitted to work. Waiting time is compensable if employee is engaged to wait (e.g., waiting for raw materials); not compensable if waiting to be engaged. On-call status is compensable if freedom of movement is restricted. Travel time: Travel from home to work is not compensable; travel that is all in a day's work, travel away from home (overnight), or travel under employer's orders is compensable.
  * **Meal Periods (Art. 85)**: Not less than 60 minutes time-off for regular meals (uncompensated). Shortened meal period to not less than 20 minutes is compensable and allowed only in non-manual work, when work is continuous, or in emergency repairs. Rest breaks of 5 to 20 minutes constitute compensable hours of work.
  * **Night Shift Differential (Art. 86)**: Minimum additional +10% of regular hourly wage for each hour of work performed between 10:00 PM and 6:00 AM.
  * **Overtime Work (Art. 87)**: Work rendered beyond 8 hours per day. Ordinary day overtime (+25% premium). Rest day or holiday overtime (+30% premium over holiday rate).
  * **Undertime Not Offset by Overtime (Art. 88)**: Express statutory ban: undertime work on any particular day shall not be offset by overtime work on any other day. Permission given to leave work early cannot be offset by overtime rendered on that or another day.
  * **Emergency Overtime (Art. 89)**: Mandatory overtime allowed only in statutory exceptions: war, local/national emergency, imminent public danger, disaster/calamity, urgent repairs to machinery/equipment to avoid serious loss, prevention of loss/damage to perishable goods, or continuous work needed to prevent serious business prejudice.
  * **Weekly Rest Day (Arts. 91–93)**: Right to consecutive 24 hours of rest after 6 consecutive days of work. Scheduling rests primarily with employer, subject to collective bargaining and employee's religious preference when employee informs employer in writing at least 7 days before. Compulsory rest day work permitted only under Art. 92 circumstances. Premium pay: 130% on scheduled rest day; 150% if rest day coincides with special non-working day.

### World 2: Statutory Holidays, Service Incentive Leave, 13th Month Pay & Service Charges
* **Statutory Basis**: Labor Code Articles 94 to 96; Presidential Decree No. 851; Republic Act No. 11360; DOLE Handbook on Workers' Statutory Benefits.
* **Comprehensive Topic Coverage**:
  * **Regular Holidays (Art. 94)**: 
    * 12 official regular holidays (New Year's Day, Maundy Thursday, Good Friday, Araw ng Kagitingan, Labor Day, Independence Day, National Heroes Day, Bonifacio Day, Christmas Day, Rizal Day, Eidul Fitr, Eidul Adha).
    * *Unworked*: 100% of daily wage (provided employee is present or on paid leave on the workday immediately preceding the holiday).
    * *Worked*: 200% for first 8 hours.
    * *Worked on Rest Day*: 260% of regular rate ($200\% \times 130\%$).
    * *Double Regular Holiday (e.g., Araw ng Kagitingan falling on Maundy Thursday)*: 200% if unworked; 300% if worked ($200\% + 100\%$ basic).
  * **Special Non-Working Days**: "No work, no pay" principle. If unworked, no pay unless company practice exists. If worked: 130% for first 8 hours; worked on rest day: 150% ($130\% + 20\%$).
  * **Service Incentive Leave (SIL, Art. 95)**: 5 days with pay annually for every employee who has rendered at least 1 year of service (12 months whether continuous or broken). Commutable to cash at the end of the year if unused. Exempt: those already enjoying 5+ days paid vacation leave, government employees, managerial staff, field personnel, and establishments regularly employing fewer than 10 workers.
  * **13th Month Pay (P.D. No. 851)**: Mandatory benefit for all rank-and-file employees regardless of status who have worked at least 1 month during the calendar year. Formula: $1/12$ of total basic salary earned during the calendar year. Must be paid not later than December 24. Exclusions from basic salary: overtime pay, night shift differential, holiday pay, unused leave conversion, maternity leave benefits, cost-of-living allowance (COLA).
  * **Service Charges Distribution (Art. 96 as amended by R.A. No. 11360)**:
    * 100% of collected service charges in hotels, restaurants, and similar establishments must be distributed completely and equally among all covered employees.
    * Equal share applies to both **rank-and-file** and **supervisory** workers.
    * Managerial employees are expressly excluded from the distribution.
    * Frequency: Paid at least once every 2 weeks or twice a month at intervals not exceeding 16 days.

### World 3: Wages, Wage Orders, Deductions & Contracting/Subcontracting
* **Statutory Basis**: Labor Code Articles 97 to 119; Republic Act No. 6727 (Wage Rationalization Act); DOLE Department Order No. 174, Series of 2017.
* **Comprehensive Topic Coverage**:
  * **Concept of Wage (Art. 97)**: Remuneration or earnings capable of being expressed in terms of money, payable by employer to employee for work done or services rendered. Facilities vs. Supplements test (*Atok-Big Wedge* doctrine): Facilities are items of expense necessary for the employee's and family's subsistence (deductible with written authorization); Supplements are extra conveniences granted for employer's benefit (not deductible).
  * **Regional Minimum Wages across 17 Regions**: Fixed by Regional Tripartite Wages and Productivity Boards (RTWPB) under R.A. 6727. Rates differ across NCR, CAR, Region I to Region XIII, and BARMM for agricultural and non-agricultural sectors.
  * **Statutory Non-Diminution of Benefits (Art. 100)**: Prohibition against employer unilaterally eliminating or diminishing benefits already granted to employees. Requirements for vested right: (1) benefit is founded on policy or continuous practice; (2) practice has been deliberate and consistent over a significant period; (3) employer knows it is not legally mandated; and (4) diminution is unilateral.
  * **Payment of Wages (Arts. 102–105)**: Legal tender only (currency, not promissory notes or tokens); payment through banks/ATM permitted; at or near workplace; paid at intervals not exceeding 16 days; direct payment to employee except in case of authorized attorney-in-fact, death, or force majeure.
  * **Lawful Wage Deductions (Art. 113)**: Strictly prohibited except: (1) insurance premiums with employee's written consent; (2) union dues with check-off authorization; (3) debts due employer where deduction is authorized by law or DOLE regulations; (4) tax withholdings and SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG contributions.
  * **Legitimate Contracting vs. Labor-Only Contracting (D.O. 174-17 / Art. 106)**:
    * *Labor-Only Contracting (LOC - Prohibited)*: Person supplying workers does not have substantial capital OR investment in the form of tools, equipment, machineries, work premises, AND the workers perform activities directly related to the principal's main business; OR the contractor does not exercise the right to control the performance of the work.
    * *Legal Effect of LOC*: Contractor is deemed a mere agent of the principal, and the principal is considered the direct employer of the workers for all intents and purposes.
    * *Substantial Capital Threshold*: Paid-up capitalization of at least **PHP 5,000,000.00** for corporations, partnerships, and cooperatives, or net worth of at least PHP 5,000,000.00 for single proprietorships.
    * *Solidary Liability*: Even in legitimate contracting, the principal is solidarily liable with the contractor for unpaid statutory minimum wages and other monetary benefits of the contractor's employees.

### World 4: Special Groups of Workers: Women, Minors, Kasambahay & Homeworkers
* **Statutory Basis**: Labor Code Articles 130 to 155; R.A. No. 10361 (Batas Kasambahay); R.A. No. 9231 (Elimination of Worst Forms of Child Labor); R.A. No. 7877 (Anti-Sexual Harassment Act); R.A. No. 11313 (Safe Spaces Act).
* **Comprehensive Topic Coverage**:
  * **Facilities for Women (Art. 130)**: Appropriate seats, separate restrooms and lavatories, dressing rooms, and lactation stations with milk storage (R.A. 10028).
  * **Prohibited Discrimination against Women (Arts. 133–135)**: Unlawful to pay a woman less wage than a man for work of equal value; unlawful to favor a male over a female regarding promotion, training, or opportunities solely on account of sex; unlawful to stipulate against marriage (*Philippine Telegraph & Telephone Co. [PT&T] v. NLRC*); unlawful to dismiss or discriminate against a woman on account of pregnancy.
  * **Employment of Children / Minors (R.A. 9231)**:
    * *Below 15 Years of Age*: Prohibited, EXCEPT: (1) work directly under the sole responsibility of parents/guardians, where only family members are employed, work does not endanger child, and DOLE work permit is secured; or (2) public entertainment or information where child's participation is essential, subject to DOLE permit. Maximum 4 hours/day and 20 hours/week; strictly no work between 8:00 PM and 6:00 AM.
    * *15 to 17 Years of Age*: Permitted in non-hazardous occupations. Maximum 8 hours/day and 40 hours/week. Strictly prohibited work between 10:00 PM and 6:00 AM.
    * *Hazardous Work*: Strictly barred for any minor under 18 years (mining, explosives, chemical exposure, heavy machinery, logging, bars/casinos).
  * **Batas Kasambahay (R.A. No. 10361)**:
    * Mandatory written employment contract registered with Punong Barangay.
    * Statutory minimum wage (regional wage boards).
    * SSS, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG mandatory coverage (employer pays 100% of contributions if salary < PHP 5,000/mo).
    * 13th month pay and 5 days annual Service Incentive Leave with pay after 1 year.
    * Daily rest: minimum 8 aggregate hours per day; weekly rest: minimum 24 consecutive hours.
    * Prohibition against loan deduction / debt bondage.
  * **Industrial Homeworkers (Arts. 151–155 / D.O. 5)**: Regulations on piece-rate payment upon delivery of goods; conditions for employer deductions; joint liability of employer and subcontractor.

### World 5: Security of Tenure, Termination of Employment & Statutory Retirement
* **Statutory Basis**: Labor Code Articles 297 to 302 [formerly Articles 279 to 287]; R.A. No. 7641; Supreme Court Landmark Jurisprudence.
* **Comprehensive Topic Coverage**:
  * **Security of Tenure (Art. 294 [279])**: Regular employee shall not be terminated except for a just or authorized cause. Illegal dismissal remedies: (1) Reinstatement without loss of seniority rights, or separation pay in lieu of reinstatement if relations are strained; (2) Full backwages inclusive of allowances and statutory benefits from time of dismissal until actual reinstatement.
  * **Four-Fold Employment Test**:
    1. Selection and engagement of the employee.
    2. Payment of wages.
    3. Power of dismissal.
    4. **Power to control employee's conduct (the most critical control test)** - control not only as to results, but as to means and methods used.
  * **Just Causes for Termination (Art. 297 [282])** - Fault of the employee (No separation pay):
    * *Serious Misconduct*: Grave and aggravated transgression connected with the work.
    * *Willful Disobedience (Insubordination)*: Refusal must be willful and order must be reasonable, lawful, and pertain to duties.
    * *Gross and Habitual Neglect of Duties*: Habitual pattern of neglect, though single gross neglect causing severe damage suffices.
    * *Fraud or Willful Breach of Trust (Loss of Confidence)*: Applies to managerial employees or fiduciary employees handling company money/property.
    * *Commission of a Crime or Offense*: Against employer, immediate family, or authorized representative.
    * *Analogous Causes*: Must be similar in gravity and character to the above.
  * **Authorized Causes for Termination (Arts. 298–299 [283–284])** - Business/Health necessity (With separation pay):
    * *Installation of Labor-Saving Devices*: Introduction of machinery replacing labor. Separation Pay: **1 month pay or at least 1 month pay per year of service**, whichever is higher.
    * *Redundancy*: Position is superfluous or in excess of actual requirements. Separation Pay: **1 month pay or at least 1 month pay per year of service**, whichever is higher.
    * *Retrenchment to Prevent Losses*: Downsizing to stave off impending substantial losses. Separation Pay: **1 month pay or at least 1/2 month pay per year of service**, whichever is higher.
    * *Closure or Cessation of Business*:
      * Not due to serious financial losses: Separation Pay: **1 month pay or at least 1/2 month pay per year of service**, whichever is higher.
      * Due to serious financial losses or bankruptcy: **No separation pay**.
    * *Disease (Art. 299 [284])*: Continued employment is prohibited by law or prejudicial to employee's health or health of co-workers. Separation Pay: **1 month pay or at least 1/2 month pay per year of service**, whichever is higher. Requires medical certification from a competent Public Health Authority that disease cannot be cured within 6 months.
  * **Procedural Due Process Requirements**:
    * *For Just Causes (Twin-Notice Rule - King of Kings Transport v. Mamac)*:
      1. **First Written Notice**: Specific grounds charged, detailed facts, directive to submit explanation within reasonable period (at least **5 calendar days**).
      2. **Hearing or Conference**: Opportunity to explain and present evidence with assistance of counsel if desired.
      3. **Second Written Notice**: Notice of termination stating all circumstances have been evaluated and just cause established.
    * *For Authorized Causes*: Written notice served on both the worker and the DOLE Regional Office at least **30 calendar days** prior to effective date.
  * **Sanctions for Defective Due Process (Agabon & Jaka Doctrines)**:
    * *Agabon v. NLRC*: Valid Just Cause + Defective Procedural Due Process = Dismissal is UPHELD (valid), but employer is ordered to pay **PHP 30,000.00 Nominal Damages**.
    * *Jaka Food Processing v. Pacot*: Valid Authorized Cause + Defective 30-Day Notice = Dismissal is UPHELD (valid), but employer is ordered to pay **PHP 50,000.00 Nominal Damages**.
  * **Statutory Retirement Pay (Art. 302 [287], R.A. No. 7641)**:
    * *Eligibility*: Age 60 optional, age 65 compulsory; minimum 5 years of service.
    * *The 22.5-Day Minimum Statutory Formula*:
      $$\text{Retirement Pay} = \text{Daily Wage} \times 22.5 \text{ days} \times \text{Years of Service}$$
      Where $22.5 \text{ days} = 15 \text{ days basic wage} + 2.5 \text{ days } (1/12 \text{ of 13th month pay}) + 5 \text{ days SIL}$.
    * Fraction of at least 6 months is credited as 1 whole year of service.

   * Enforces dark slate text (`#0F172A`) on light/pastel backgrounds and bright text (`#FFFFFF`) on dark backgrounds, guaranteeing $\Delta \text{Lum} \ge 0.42$ across every header, cell, and masthead.
3. **Pure Trigonometric Vector Graphics (Zero Unicode Emojis)**:
   * Avoids ugly unrendered Unicode boxes or missing glyphs by rendering Super Stars, Question Blocks, Warp Pipes, Scales of Justice, and Bricks using native trigonometric `jsPDF` vector paths (`doc.lines`, `doc.circle`, `doc.roundedRect`).

## Local Development & Setup

### Prerequisites
* **Node.js**: Version 18.x or higher (tested on Node v20 and v22 LTS)
* **npm**: Version 9.x or higher

### Step-by-Step Installation
1. Clone the repository:
   ```bash
   git clone https://github.com/minjeuuu/LawMario.git
   cd LawMario
   ```

2. Install dependencies:
   ```bash
   npm install
   ```

3. Launch the development server:
   ```bash
   npm run dev
   ```
   Open your browser at `http://localhost:3000`.

4. Run the comprehensive unit test suite:
   ```bash
   npm test
   ```

5. Build for production:
   ```bash
   npm run build
   ```

## Deployment to Vercel

This repository is optimized for one-click deployment on **Vercel**:

1. Push your repository to your GitHub account.
2. Visit [Vercel](https://vercel.com) and click **Add New Project**.
3. Import your `LawMario` repository.
4. Vercel automatically detects the framework preset as **Vite**:
   * **Build Command**: `npm run build`
   * **Output Directory**: `dist`
5. Click **Deploy**. The included `vercel.json` ensures all SPA client-side routes, PWA service workers, and static assets route smoothly without 404 errors.
